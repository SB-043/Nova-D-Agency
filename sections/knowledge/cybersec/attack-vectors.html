Understanding Attack Vectors

An introduction to common attack vectors, how they are exploited, and how organizations can reduce their exposure.

Beginner Updated 2025 Nova Digital

What Is an Attack Vector?

An attack vector is a path or method used by attackers to gain unauthorized access to a system, network, or application.

Common Attack Vectors

Phishing & Social Engineering

Phishing and social engineering attacks manipulate human behavior rather than exploiting technical flaws. Attackers impersonate trusted entities through emails, messages, or phone calls to trick victims into revealing credentials, downloading malware, or granting access.

Exposed Services & Open Ports

Services exposed to the internet without proper access controls increase attack surface. Open ports running outdated or unnecessary services can be discovered through scanning and exploited for initial access.

Unpatched Software Vulnerabilities

Software vulnerabilities arise when systems are not updated with security patches. Attackers frequently exploit known CVEs to gain unauthorized access or escalate privileges.

Misconfigured Cloud Resources

Cloud misconfigurations such as public storage buckets, weak IAM policies, or exposed APIs can lead to data leaks and full environment compromise.

Malicious File Uploads

Allowing users to upload files without strict validation can result in malware uploads, web shells, or remote code execution if files are improperly handled or executed on the server.

Why Attack Vectors Matter

Understanding attack vectors helps defenders prioritize controls, reduce risk, and design layered security architectures.
